A record doesn't move because someone posted it. It moves because a machine of small, reliable jobs runs around it: watching playlists, keeping press assets current, clearing samples before they become lawsuits, answering questions about the catalog, and keeping receipts of the work that was actually done.

That machine is what we built. Five tools, each one narrow, each one live, each one free to use. Not a demo. Not a pitch deck. Running software on GitHub Pages, tested and deployed, built at zero cost.

Playlist Radar watches the playlists that matter. It tracks where a track sits, when it moves, and when it disappears. Playlist placements are the oxygen of an independent release — an add to a curator list can mean thousands of plays, and losing one without noticing is how momentum quietly dies. Radar turns that into a dashboard instead of a daily manual scroll: positions over time, verified positions, no guessing.

Living Press Kits solve the oldest problem in promo: the PDF that goes stale. A press kit that says a track has 200,000 plays when it actually has 300,000 is worse than no press kit at all — it tells a journalist you don't know your own numbers. Living Press Kits update themselves. Stream counts, placements, press hits, bios — the kit reads live data and renders it fresh every time someone opens it. The journalist always sees the truth, and the truth is always current.

Sample Clearance handles the unglamorous work that kills careers when skipped. Clearance is a workflow: identify what needs clearing, who owns it, what the terms are, and where the paperwork stands. This tool structures that workflow so nothing lives in a DM thread or a forgotten email. For an independent operation doing everything in-house, that's the difference between shipping and getting shelved.

Catalog Chat gives the catalog a voice. Fifty-plus tracks across multiple artists, ISRCs, credits, placements, rights — nobody remembers all of it, and every inquiry ("who produced this?", "what's the ISRC?", "is this cleared for sync?") burns somebody's time. Catalog Chat answers from verified catalog data, not from a model's memory. It reads the same machine-readable records our public endpoints serve and answers in plain language.

IG Receipts keeps receipts of promo work. Every post, every playlist add, every press hit — logged with proof. The rule that runs this company is "verified or it didn't happen," and IG Receipts is the enforcement mechanism: a claim about promo activity has to point at a receipt, or it doesn't go in the report.

Every tool in the stack ships under the same operating doctrine: name the result it produces, measure it, and carry a numeric kill rule. Playlist Radar, Living Press Kits, and Sample Clearance each carry a December 18, 2026 kill date with a 75% checkpoint on November 25 — if the numbers don't hold at the checkpoint, the lane gets reworked or cut. Software that can't justify its own existence doesn't survive the quarter.

The stack is honest infrastructure for an independent label running without a major's budget. Nothing here pretends to replace a team; everything here is built so a small team moves like a bigger one.
